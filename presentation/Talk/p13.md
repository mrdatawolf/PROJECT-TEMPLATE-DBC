And I want to be precise about what's borrowed and what isn't, because
this is the part people mishear. Meyer's contracts are Boolean assertions
attached to routines and classes, and in Eiffel they can be checked
automatically at runtime. What I'm describing here is not that. Same
purpose — make obligations explicit — completely different enforcement
mechanism: mostly human-readable contracts, tasks, and acceptance
criteria, enforced by tests, independent review, workflow controls, and
final human acceptance. I am not claiming process documents are executable
assertions. That would be a bad claim and also not true. What I am
claiming is that the discipline of "make the obligation explicit before
you rely on it" transfers cleanly one level up, from function boundaries
to human/agent boundaries.
