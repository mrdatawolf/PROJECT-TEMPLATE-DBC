And I want to be precise about what's borrowed and what isn't, because
this is the part people might mishear. Meyer's contracts are Boolean 
assertions attached to routines and classes, and in Eiffel they can be 
checked automatically at runtime. What I'm describing is not that. It 
has the same purpose — to make obligations explicit — but with a 
completely different enforcement mechanism. Specifically via 
human-readable contracts, tasks, acceptance criteria, enforced by tests,
independent review, workflow controls, and final human acceptance. I am 
not claiming process documents are executable assertions. I am claiming 
that the discipline of "make the obligation explicit before you rely on 
it" transfers cleanly one level up, from function boundaries to 
human/agent boundaries.
