## What is old is new

[Slide 2 — "Building got cheap. Deciding what to build didn't."]

This is actually a three-act story, not a before/after. Punch-card
programmers thought hard before they ever touched a keyboard, because a
wasted run cost you the rest of the day — the discipline was forced on
them by how expensive an attempt was. Then computing got interactive,
attempts got cheap, and cheap attempts let teams skip the thinking and
debug their way to something that worked, fixing the design later, if
ever. If you've ever shipped a hotfix that outlived the "temporary"
workaround by three years, you already know which side of that trade
you've been living on.

AI agents, coding, pushes execution past cheap and all the way into disposable. 
This puts us back to where the punch-card programmers were — thinking
is the hard part, again, but for the opposite reason. Before, attempts being
scarce drove it, now, it is because everything except deciding what's correct got
free. Same discipline, from the opposite direction.
