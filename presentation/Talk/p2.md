## What is old is new

[Slide 2 — "Building got cheap. Deciding what to build didn't."]

I suggest that where we are today is the end of a three-act story, not 
a before AI/after AI. Punch-card programmers thought hard before they ever 
touched a keyboard, because a wasted run cost you the rest of the day — 
the discipline was forced on them by how expensive an attempt was. Then 
computing got interactive, attempts got cheap, and cheap attempts let teams skip 
the thinking and debug their way to something that worked, fixing the design 
"later". If you've shipped a "temporary" workaround that never got refactored, 
you already know what I am talking about.

AI agents coding, pushes execution past cheap and all the way into disposable. 
This puts us back to where the punch-card programmers were — thinking
is the hard part, again, but for the opposite reason. Before, attempts being
scarce drove it, now, it is because everything except deciding what's correct got
cheap. Same discipline, from the opposite direction.
