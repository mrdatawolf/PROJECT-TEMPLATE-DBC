If you want the direct translation: precondition becomes approved scope,
dependencies, and required context — what authorizes implementation to
start. Postcondition becomes "at handoff, the implementation satisfies the
contract and its observable acceptance criteria." Invariant becomes
"unrelated behavior and user changes stay preserved at reviewable
boundaries." Interface becomes a contract describing required behavior
without prescribing implementation details it doesn't need to. Contract
checking becomes independent review verifying the claimed postconditions
and invariants, with human acceptance kept as a separate governance
decision — review and acceptance are not the same gate.

One nuance worth flagging for this crowd specifically: ambiguity
escalation belongs to contract design, not to runtime exception handling.
An unclear contract isn't "ready for implementation with a caveat" — it's
not ready, full stop. That's a different posture than "catch and handle,"
and it's deliberate.
